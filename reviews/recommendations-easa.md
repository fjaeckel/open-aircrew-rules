# Recommendations for the EASA decisions

Summary: 38 EASA needs-decision rows from the decision table in [README.md](README.md). The recommendation keeps the current choice in 8 rows and changes it in 30. Low confidence: the three medical `counted-from` rows and the four towing rows.
Evidence is limited to the Regulation text in `sources/easa/`, EASA AMC/GM, EASA opinions, NPAs and FAQs, and national authority material. All of it is summarised in our own words and cited by paragraph. Nothing here is approved: `approved_by` stays with the signing reviewer.

Principles (proposed by the owner): P1 missing record data that decides whether the pilot may fly is reported unknown rather than counted; P2 only a check recorded for this credential counts; P3 count within the validity period, not "any time before expiry"; P4 simulators and devices count only where the text permits it; P5 presentation-only rows keep the current choice. "text" means that the wording of the rule or its official guidance decides the row without a principle. A row marked "As for X" follows X.

Guidance searched: the Easy Access Rules for Aircrew (November 2025 revision, which includes the Part-MED AMC/GM), the Easy Access Rules for Sailplanes, Opinion No 05/2023 with its Annex II rationale, NPA 2020-14, the EASA aircrew and medical FAQ pages, and the LBA and Austro Control licensing pages. Several of the questions have no official guidance. Where that is so, the evidence column says it.

## Findings beyond the listed options

- **Class 1 validity for lower privileges.** AMC1 MED.A.030(a) treats a class 1 certificate as including the class 2 and LAPL validities. A class 1 certificate whose 12 (or 6) months have run out therefore still supports class 2 or LAPL privileges until the class 2 or LAPL period ends. Today `easa.medical.class-1` derives only the class 1 period, and `easa.shared.medical-certificate` accepts a class 1 certificate only by its recorded expiry. As a result, a PPL, LAPL or SPL holder, or a CPL holder flying on PPL privileges, who holds a class 1 certificate is reported without a valid medical after 12 months. This matters as soon as the `class-1-only` rows move to option (2) or (3).
- **FI(As) and FI(G) hours.** Whatever is decided on `instruction-any-category`, holding an FI(As) to 50 hours rather than 20 contradicts FCL.940.FI(a)(1)(i)(B).
- **CRI single-engine and multi-engine split.** FCL.940.CRI(a)(1) requires the hours to be split equally when a CRI holds both single-engine and multi-engine privileges. The record holds the class of each flight, so this could be evaluated. The file does not check it.
- **SFCL.160 month-end counting.** AMC1 SFCL.160(a)(1)(ii)(d) counts the 24 months from the last day of the month of the training flight. `easa.licence.spl` has no month-end rule for this item. This is not one of the listed rows, but the SPL reviewer should check it.
